# Timestep-Adaptive Fast Johnson–Lindenstrauss Cache for Diffusion Transformers
> ⚠️ **NOTE:** This research is an automatic research using AIRAS.
## Abstract
Diffusion Transformers currently deliver state-of-the-art visual quality but their inference cache can exceed the memory budget of commodity GPUs, especially for video generation. Existing compressors apply a fixed low-rank projection and precision to every denoising step, store dense projection matrices, ignore frame-to-frame correlation, rely on unsupported odd-bit arithmetic and provide no principled mechanism for respecting a user-defined quality budget. We introduce TA-FJLT-Cache, a training-free and analytically scheduled pipeline that replaces dense projectors with a matrix-free Fast Johnson–Lindenstrauss Transform, chooses rank and quantisation bits per timestep as a direct function of the predicted noise variance, exploits both denoising-step and real-time deltas, refreshes accuracy through periodic key-frames with residual replay and relies only on native INT4 and INT8 operations. A variance-preserving analysis yields an inequality linking rank and bit-width to a per-step error budget and shows that the cumulative budget provably bounds the degradation in Fréchet scores. On DiT-XL/2 and U-ViT-H/2 for images and on PixArt-Sigma-S for video, TA-FJLT-Cache reduces peak VRAM by 12–15× relative to vanilla caching and by roughly 1.3× over the strongest prior, adds 5 % FLOPs with negligible latency for images and even speeds up video inference thanks to larger feasible batch sizes. Quality remains within a user-specified FID/FVD tolerance, and empirical degradation correlates with the analytic prediction (r²≈0.93). Dual-axis delta coding enables 16-frame 256² video generation under a strict 8 GB cap with <1 % CLIP-Score loss, demonstrating a practical path toward affordable high-quality generative inference.
